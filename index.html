About

What I work on

My research lives at the interface of polymer chemistry and biology: synthesizing and functionalizing amide-based polymers, then building hydrogels, sponges, and adhesives out of them and testing whether they actually do something useful in a biological setting. My MS thesis centers on enzymatically crosslinked polymeric sponges for hemostatic applications — porous, bioactive networks that combine rapid blood absorption with clot formation, including work on polymer–metal ion interactions to enhance clotting and tissue response. Before that, sponge-phase lipids, biomedical adhesives, and metalloenzyme model chemistry, each adding a different piece of how molecular design becomes material behavior. Longer term, I'm interested in what it takes to move biomaterials like these from the lab bench toward real healthcare use — hemostasis, wound healing, and regenerative medicine broadly.

Research

Projects

Polymeric Sponges for Hemostatic Applications

MS Thesis · Dr. Aasheesh Srivastava · IISER Bhopal

Functionalized amide-based (polysuccinimide-derived) polymers, crosslinked into hydrogels via enzyme-mediated and photocrosslinking routes, then freeze-dried into porous, bioactive sponges. Investigated polymer–metal ion interactions to enhance blood clotting and tissue response. Work spanned PEC-based adhesives, electrospun nanofibers, sponges, and foams, characterized by rheology, FTIR, UV–Vis, and SEM, and evaluated for self-healing behavior, blood compatibility, and in vitro hemostatic performance. Led a team through the accompanying cytocompatibility, antibacterial, and in vivo studies.

Patent filing in progress
